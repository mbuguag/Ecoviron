Workplace Hygiene & Survey Audits

Our goal is to promote employee health, prevent work-related illnesses, and ensure compliance with national health and safety regulations.

Overview and Legal Requirements

Workplace hygiene audits are guided by the Occupational Safety and Health Act, 2007 (OSHA), and supported by various subsidiary regulations. These laws require employers to maintain a clean, safe, and healthy environment for workers.

  • OSHA Section 6(1): Requires employers to ensure the health, safety, and welfare of employees at work.
  • OSHA Section 13(1): Requires development of workplace policies that include hygiene standards.
  • OSHA Section 74: Requires cleanliness of workplaces including floors, walls, and workstations.
  • Factories (Cleanliness, Ventilation, and Lighting) Rules, Legal Notice No. 44 of 2004: Specific requirements on hygiene, lighting, air circulation, and waste handling.

Our Process

At Bionix ESH Solutions, our hygiene audits follow a structured and compliant process:

  1. Initial Site Visit and Engagement

    We assess the facility layout and operational processes to determine audit needs.

  2. Documentation and Records Review

    We review cleaning schedules, pest control logs, sanitation records, hygiene SOPs, and staff policies.

  3. Physical Hygiene Survey
    • Cleanliness of floors, walls, and work surfaces
    • Handwashing stations, restrooms, and changing rooms
    • Indoor air quality and ventilation
    • Break/food handling areas
    • Waste management practices
    • Staff hygiene and PPE usage
  4. Employee Consultation

    We interview employees to understand hygiene challenges, compliance awareness, and feedback.

  5. Report Compilation and Recommendations

    We provide a detailed report identifying risks and offering practical, actionable recommendations.

  6. Post-Audit Support

    We assist in corrective actions, updating SOPs, and preparing for regulatory inspections.

Why This Audit Is Done

  • Prevent disease outbreaks and occupational illnesses
  • Promote employee wellbeing and morale
  • Ensure a clean and safe working environment
  • Comply with OSHA and public health laws
  • Avoid penalties from DOSHS and county health departments
